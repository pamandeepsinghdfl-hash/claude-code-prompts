## LinkedIn (founder launch story)
Why we built Hang — and why "offline" is the feature, not the fallback.

Over the past year building this, one thing kept coming up in every conversation: people don't feel short on connections, they feel short on *people nearby*. We're more "connected" than any generation in history and somehow worse at just running into each other.

Hang started from a simple, almost dumb question we asked strangers on the street: "What are you actually doing right now — and would you want someone nearby to know?" The honest answers (study groups, yoga mats, a coffee nobody wanted to finish alone) became the product.

Hang is a live map. You set what you're doing and how far you're willing to be found. When someone near you is doing the same thing, we surface it — we call that a Spark. Then you push-to-talk, say hi, and go meet them. No feed to scroll, no follower count to chase. The entire design goal is to get you to close the app and go outside.

We're not trying to maximize time-in-app. We're trying to minimize it — by making the one thing that happens inside it worth leaving for.

Launching soon. If "more real meetups, fewer doom-scrolls" resonates with you, I'd love your thoughts.
