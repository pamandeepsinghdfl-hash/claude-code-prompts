# Viral street-interview format — research findings → concrete changes for the trailer

Researched via web search (not invented). Caveat up front: most specific numbers below
come from vendor/creator blogs with no cited methodology — treat percentages as
directional, not fact. Sourced per point.

## What to actually change in the current trailer

1. **Open on the most interesting answer fragment, not a title card.** A street-interview
   editing case study recommends opening on ~3 seconds of the single most intriguing
   moment (max "knowledge gap"), then backfilling context — not starting at the logical
   beginning. *Change:* cut the smoking-interview punchline ("Taking five. Don't tell my
   mom.") or the science one ("not even kidding") to the very front, before the "ONE
   QUESTION. ZERO FILTERS." hook card, then let the hook card land second as context.
   ([Contra case study](https://contra.com/p/WZbLOYBY-viral-street-interviews-and-field-content-creation))

2. **Lead each beat with the answer, caption the question back in on-screen text —
   don't show the question first.** A short-form editing tutorial specifically argues
   against opening a cut on the interviewer's question. *Change:* in each of our 6 cards,
   put the quote first/biggest, and shrink "Drop the pin?" to a secondary caption line
   (closer to what we already do) rather than leading with the category label.
   ([editing retention trick](https://nowbam.com/?p=162209))

3. **Keep cuts fast and soundbites short.** Vox-pop curricula converge on quick edits,
   short bites, usually under 2 minutes total, 10+ distinct voices for enough material to
   cut between. *Change:* our 6 cards at ~4.3s each (26s total) is already in range; don't
   stretch them longer. Consider trimming to 4 strongest cards (smoking, coffee/chill,
   science, spirituality) at ~3.5s each if we want a snappier cut.
   ([KQED vox pop curriculum](https://www.kqed.org/education/vox-pop-curriculum))

4. **Captions: 3-5 words per on-screen chunk, bold sans-serif, high contrast.** Multiple
   caption guides converge on short word groups over full sentences, with a stroke/shadow
   for legibility over busy backgrounds. *Change:* our quote cards currently show full
   sentences at once — split long quotes (the yoga and coffee ones run 2 lines) into
   sequential word-group reveals instead of appearing all at once.
   ([caption styles guide](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/social-media-captions-styles-a-complete-guide/))

5. **CTA should land after value, not just at the very end, and must be captioned
   on-screen (most viewers watch muted).** Guidance suggests placing the CTA around the
   15-20s mark *and* again at the close, as on-screen text, never audio-only.
   *Change:* our current structure delivers "Coming soon" only once, at 72s. Add a
   lightweight mid-roll CTA card right after the app-demo section ("Sound like you? →")
   before the push-to-talk beat, in addition to the closer.
   ([caption/CTA placement](https://www.opus.pro/blog/monetizing-videos-with-strategic-caption-placement-and-cta-integration))

6. **Don't over-polish the audio — keep it feeling real, not studio.** The Contra case
   study specifically recommends preserving some ambient noise rather than clean studio
   audio, because over-polished street interviews read as fake. Relevant to us since we
   can't film real people: lean into the HUD/synth aesthetic rather than trying to fake
   documentary realism.
   ([Contra case study](https://contra.com/p/WZbLOYBY-viral-street-interviews-and-field-content-creation))

## Gaps / things no source actually confirmed
